# The free reply, checked against T3

On the 2026-11-20 call the voice told the caller a thread would now proceed when it had sent nothing and the permission request was still pending (a free reply, nothing drafted or checked), could not answer that request by voice at all, and repeated a withdrawn question's candidates. This page records the three fixes, what they were measured at, and what is not proven. Mason's ruling of 2026-11-20 reverses the log-only rule of [live-fact-check.md](live-fact-check.md) for one kind of claim: a free reply about a thread is now corrected.

## Check a free reply that claims something about a thread (`voice/live/replycheck.py`)

The reply is spoken as before, with nothing held. When its text is complete (`response.done`), the local d1-2B (the summary gate's server, on a connection of its own) reads one typed choice, `Decider.claims` in `voice/t3/verify.py`: what the reply says about a coding thread, against what T3 holds for the thread this call last spoke about. That is the thread's state and pending request, the end of its last reply, and what the voice itself sent to it or stopped (the seam's read carries one reply and no message history, and its record of recent messages was backed out in PR #66, so the voice's own journal stands for "what was sent"). `unsupported` at 0.5 or more flags the reply. With no thread in view, nothing is checked.

### When the text is there, and why the check is not started on partial text

Measured on 12 free replies of the 2026-11-20 call's records (events only): the last text delta came a median 0.68 s after `response.create` (0.07 to 0.88), `response.done` and the text's `output_text.done` together a median 1.11 s (0.31 to 1.36), and the audio played for a median 6.16 s (1.84 to 8.32). So the complete text exists 0.97 s or more before the audio ends, a median 5.27 s. The last text delta comes about 0.3 s before `response.done` (0.61 against 0.91 s on the reply that wrongly said the thread would proceed), but nothing marks it as the last until `output_text.done`. A check on partial text could start about 0.3 to 0.4 s earlier, against a check of about 44 ms and a verdict that lands 0.05 to 0.5 s after the text, so it was not built.

### What the caller hears

| Where the caller is when the verdict lands                      | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The reply is still playing, the caller is quiet                 | The rest of its audio is dropped through the barge-in path (`Conversation.stop_playback`, the same call `start_turn` makes), a short holding line is spoken, then the correction                                                                                                                                                      |
| The reply has finished playing, the caller is quiet             | The holding line and the correction are spoken as a follow-up                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| The caller is already talking, or the reply was cut off by them | Nothing is spoken over them. The correction waits for a quiet moment, like thread news (`hold_news`), and only if what they heard before the cut still held an unsupported claim: the check runs again on the words that played (the share of the reply's audio heard, as words). A reply cut off before its claim is never corrected |

The correction is a typed act, `correction` (thread, status, what it waits on), worded by the text model, slot-checked and decider-checked, and said in the call voice like every T3 line. The holding line covers the time that takes. It is a free reply to a private note (`run.TRANSITION_NOTE`) that carries only the meaning, so the voice words it each time. If the correction is already ready when the flag lands, nothing changes: it never is, because the correction's draft starts at the flag, so the holding line is always said first and the draft is made while it plays (`say_acts(lead=...)`). The holding line is not itself checked against T3, and the voice has made up a fact in it once in 12 trials of one wording (see below).

`ReplyCheck` takes the live server's view of the caller as callbacks, so every row above is a unit test (`voice/live/tests/test_replycheck.py`) and the turn flow is one too (`voice/prototype/tests/test_note_mode.py`).

These are from the committed recording, made on the CPU. The first recording, on the GPU, had 8 of 10 and 0 of 10 on the seen set and 8 of 9 and 2 of 11 on the held-out set. It was redone because two made-up cases were worded too close to the call's own reply and thread title and were changed (one seen case now scores 0.46 and is not flagged), and the GPU was held by another job. On the 35 requests that did not change, CPU and GPU unsupported shares differ by at most 0.022 (mean 0.005), and one case moves across the bar (a time of day: 0.51 on the GPU, 0.49 on the CPU), which is the difference between 2 and 1 held-out false flags. The held-out set was in effect scored twice. The held-out false flags are replies that are not about a thread's state: a joke about wishing it could clear the request (0.58) and, on the GPU recording, a time of day (0.51). A false flag interrupts a correct reply and then says it was wrong, so this is the error that matters. The missed wrong replies were a vague "it's done" (0.43), "I stopped it for you" (0.31) and a reply that the thread would carry on (0.46) on the seen set, and "I passed your follow-up along to it just now" (0.25) on the held-out one. The first prompt (two yes/no questions) flagged 0 of 10 wrong replies on the seen set at its natural bar; the choice question with the stricter context was the second arm tried. A check takes a median 40.6 ms (p95 45.5 ms) on the 40 recorded cases, and a median 43.5 ms (max 68 ms) over the 59 rehearsal checks.

On the rehearsal (`python -m voice.live.rehearse drive --scenario claims`, the real server, five calls, scripted T3, `--decider d1`; the free replies below were read by hand): 59 free replies were checked, 19 had no thread in view and were not, 38 were not flagged and 2 were. Of the replies I read as unsupported (about 5) the check flagged 2 and missed 3 at 0.44, 0.37 and 0.19. The two flags were corrected after the caller had heard 1.07 s of a 7.28 s reply and 0.98 s of a 5.84 s one (the verdict lands 0.05 to 0.19 s after the text), so the correction beat the end of playback 2 of 2 and about 15% of the audio had played. Flag to first audio of the holding line: 0.25 and 0.26 s (2 samples). Flag to first audio of the correction: 2.05 s once, and once the correction line was refused twice and never spoken (see unproven). The verdict lands a median 0.095 s after the text (p90 0.50 s).

First audio on ordinary chat turns, from speech end, measured on the same chat lines across the three rehearsal scenarios: unchanged code 1.321 s median (p90 1.604 s, 67 turns), this code 1.344 s (p90 1.701 s, 57 turns). The check starts after the text is complete, so it cannot delay first audio; the 23 ms is the spread of two runs, not shown to be an effect.

## Answer a pending permission request by voice

`respond` is a new seam operation (`apps/server/src/voiceSeam/respond.ts`): it answers one pending approval of a visible thread with T3's `runtime-request.respond` command, the web client's own path (`respondToThreadApproval`), as `accept` or `decline` and never "for the session" or "always". The request id the voice mints fixes the command, a retry answers from its receipt, and the request must still be pending on that thread and be an approval: a question, a card and a request only the app can take are refused. It is off unless the seam's config lists `respond` in `operations` (the sandbox's does; Mason's own `voice-seam.json` does not until he adds it).

The decider chooses with two tools, `t3_approve` and `t3_deny`, offered only while a thread waits on the person for an approval, and told which (`T3Registry.approvals`). The confirmation names the thread and what is being approved in a few words (a command's program, never the command line), a yes sends the answer once, and the line afterwards says what T3 returned: its answer, its refusal, or that it could not be reached. A yes never answers a request it did not name: it re-reads the thread, and if the request changed it asks about the new one. After a restart an unanswered confirmation is never replayed.

More than one pending request: one is answered at a time. With several threads waiting the voice asks which, offering only the threads that wait; with one, there is nothing to choose; a thread's read carries one pending request, and the next is asked about afresh. A thread whose title another thread shares is not offered (the voice says so, as for a stop). "Approve all" is not supported.

Tool choice on 26 made-up cases (`python -m voice.t3.eval.tool_choice`), once per decider:

| Decider                                                                                                                       | Approve | Deny   | Status question | Chat, claim-bait, withdraw | Approve or deny said with nothing waiting |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ------ | --------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Hosted d1.2 (the calls' decider)                                                                                              | 5 of 5  | 4 of 4 | 4 of 4          | 11 of 11                   | 2 of 2                                    |
| Local d1-2B, from the final rehearsal's picks (not the 26 cases: that run was left queued behind the evals worker's GPU hold) | 3 of 4  | 0 of 1 | 4 of 4          | not counted                | not run                                   |

Without the sentence added to the request criterion in `voice/segmentation/hybrid.py` (`T3_APPROVAL`), hosted routed "Go ahead and allow it." to `t3_approve` and `t3_ask` both (4 of 5 approve), so it stays; it, and the examples in the two tool lines, were first added for the local decider and were not ablated one at a time on the hosted one. Putting the waiting list in the shared decider state moved the local d1-2B's read of ordinary status questions to chat (5 of 5), so it is not there.

The confirmation line is checked by the local d1-2B for claiming the request is already approved. The first wording read "Should I approve X running its tests?" as a claim at 0.61 and refused it twice. With the question reworded ("as something that has happened") and the bar for that one claim at 0.75, on 8 made-up lines (4 good confirmations, 4 that claim an answer) 7 were right: no good line refused, and one false "I've approved X running its tests, right?" passed (it still needs a yes to act). That sample is too small to set a bar on.

Rehearsal (`--scenario approve`, five calls, local d1-2B as the decider), the scripted T3's received answers: unchanged code 0 of 5 calls, with free replies such as "I'll proceed with the approval"; this code 3 of 5 (calls whose approve was picked and confirmed), each naming the thread and what it runs, each answered with the right decision. Not working on the local decider: a deny read as chat and answered "I will not allow that" in free text (and was not checked, because the status line of that call had been refused, so no thread was in view), and "I approve." with two threads waiting read as a status question. The PM's ruling is that the local d1-2B is not the call's decider, so no more tuning was done for it.

## A withdrawn question stops being asked

The voice session holds a relayed question's words as plain history, and llama.cpp cannot remove an item. On the 2026-11-20 call a turn that dropped the question without a line (the caller's "never mind" read as clarify, no T3 entry) had the voice model answer freely, and its answer listed the candidates again, five turns in a row. A turn that drops a question the caller had heard, without answering it, now says so to the voice model once, as a private note after the audio (`WITHDRAWN_NOTE`): they did not answer your question and moved on, answer what they said now, do not ask it again. The note names no choice. `T3Tasks.take_withdrawn` reports it once (an answered question, a question nobody heard, and one from another call do not).

Not shown to help: the repeat did not reproduce on the unchanged code in the rehearsal (`--scenario withdraw`, five calls): 0 of 15 free replies after "never mind" named an offered thread. With the note, 4 of 4 dropped questions sent it and 0 of 15 named one, so there is no before and after. The one case where the voice asked "which thread" again after a "no, never mind" was a question that had never been heard, which the design asks again on purpose.

## The `same_title` stop line

A stop for a title two threads share is refused and the voice says so. Its line was refused twice ("call reply missing audio or incomplete"). Not the third-person "they" in the draft: the call voice opened its reply with a refusal of its own ("I can't help", "There are multiple threads") on that line in any person, and the response stops at its first word off the line (`relay_eval --set same_title`, 8 trials of each): "which one they mean" 1 of 8 recited first and 4 of 8 on the retry, "which one you mean" 1 and 4, the example's own shape 2 and 5. A line that opens on what was done ("I didn't stop anything, because more than one thread is called X.") recited 8 of 8, one that opens "I can't tell which X thread to stop" 8 of 8, and "Two threads share the name X, so I didn't stop either one" 8 of 8, first relay and retry (`--set same_title_forms`). The writer copies the shape of its example, so the example is the first, and the direction to the writer no longer says "they". The repo's copies of these lines were given a made-up title after the runs (the runs used the call's own title), and were not run again.

## The notes' length instruction

The holding line for a running lookup ended on "in a few words", which the call's voice once said as content (a lookup line that included the length instruction). The lookup, result and dropped notes, the ask-instead note and the interrupted note ended on a length instruction too. They no longer do; the persona already asks for one or two short sentences. Not reproduced: `note_eval` (12 trials per note, a fresh session) had no reply say a length phrase on the old wording either (0 of 12 each), and the median words per reply were 6, 16 and 7 for the old notes and 7, 15 and 9 for the new, so the change is unproven. The hosted-call's context may be what it needs.

## Unproven

- The correction line was refused twice and never spoken in one rehearsal (the writer's two drafts failed the check), so that turn's wrong claim was left standing and given back for a quiet moment. Not diagnosed.
- Fault 3 (the repeated candidates) did not reproduce on the unchanged code, so the note's effect is not measured; only that it fires on every dropped question.
- The check misses wrong replies (about 3 of 5 in the rehearsal, 1 of 9 and 3 of 10 on the made-up sets) and falsely flags some (1 of 11 held out on the CPU recording, 2 of 11 on the GPU one).
- Approve and deny were exercised against a scripted seam and a fake T3 service only. The TypeScript operation is tested with scripted services (`respond.test.ts`), not against a live T3 with a pending approval.
- A reply that claims something about a thread with no thread in view is not checked.
- Rehearsals ran the local d1-2B as the call's decider, which is not what calls use; the hosted decider was measured on tool choice only.
